I tested 788 Canadian websites. The average Google speed score: 54.9/100.

September 23, 2026 · 4 min read

Over the past few days I ran the same mobile speed test Google itself publishes on 788 Canadian business websites — dental clinics, heating/air-conditioning companies, real estate agents, optometrists, and physiotherapy clinics from Vancouver to St. John's, across 46 cities.

The results are surprisingly consistent. Here they are, with no spin:

  • Average score: 54.9/100. On the mobile test, on a simulated phone, on slow 4G — exactly how customers see your site.
  • About 41% of sites scored under 50 — the "red" zone on Google's own scale.
  • Only about 4% reached green (90+) — 32 sites out of 788.
  • Best score: 100. Worst: 6.

To be fair about a few things: Google's score varies a bit from run to run, so treat these as estimates — the same way Google itself does. A handful of sites wouldn't answer the test at all (dozens of attempts came back empty — dead servers, blocked testing bots, or sites that time out). And every site was tested the honest way: homepage, phone, slow 4G — the way a customer actually arrives.

What a score under 50 means for your business

It means someone on a phone — usually in a hurry, usually ready to buy — stared at a blank or half-loaded screen while tapping their foot.

A furnace dies at midnight. Nobody browses ad-free and compares review sites at -30 °C. The homeowner searches, and calls the first website that loads. If your page takes 9 seconds, the faster competitor down the street already picked up.

Why are these sites slow?

Four things show up again and again (details in Why is my website slow?):

  • Images sent to phones at full desktop size — the #1 cause.
  • Scripts blocking the page — chat widgets, trackers, font loaders that make the browser wait.
  • Slow server response — shared hosting with no caching or compression.
  • Caching off — returning visitors re-download everything, every time.

None of these need a redesign. None of them are "your website is bad." They're settings and habits — fixable, usually in a day or two.

Your three options

  1. Ignore it. The trend is against you — mobile speed is a ranking signal and a buying signal at the same time.
  2. Guess. Ask someone to "make it faster" without numbers. That's how you pay for polish and keep the slowness.
