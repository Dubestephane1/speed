Which industry has the fastest websites in Canada? The 2026 niche ranking

September 25, 2026 · 6 min read

I audit Canadian business websites for free at speed.stephanedube.dev. So far: 788 sites — dental clinics, heating and air-conditioning companies, real estate agents, optometrists, and physiotherapy clinics.

Every city ranking I've published pools niches together. But industries can't hide from the same test: the real Google PageSpeed mobile score, out of 100. So which trade builds the fastest websites in Canada?

Spoiler: it's not the flashiest one. Here's the whole ranking, with every site counted once.

The story the numbers tell

  • HVAC wins. Heating, air-conditioning, and plumbing companies — usually independent tradespeople with straightforward websites — have the fastest sites in the study: 58/100 on average, the only industry under 40% failing, and a 98 ceiling.
  • Physiotherapy is close behind — and owns the only perfect score in all 788 audits (100/100). It also has the highest share of green sites (7%).
  • Real estate is the flashiest niche and the most deceived. Mid-table on average, but it holds the worst real-estate website in the study — an 8/100 in Vancouver (the global worst of 6/100 belongs to the dental niche). Listing feeds, sliders, and image galleries are a classic recipe for a slow page.
  • Optometrists are middle of the pack — not winners. 47% failing is the second-highest failure rate, though their best site (99/100) shows what the niche is capable of.
  • Dental is last, and it's the biggest sample. Over half of the 234 dental websites fail Google's own threshold. That's the most fixable market in the study — and I've spent the most time in it.
  • Across all 788 sites, only 32 reach green (90+). Five niches, five different stories — but one shared problem.

HVAC — 58/100 · 205 sites

The quiet winner. These are heating, ventilation, air-conditioning, and plumbing companies — independent businesses running no-nonsense sites with fewer scripts and trackers than corporate brochureware. The fast half of the sample comes from province-wide lists, and the best site scores 98/100.

Honest caveat: every HVAC city sample I have is small (3–4 sites each — Calgary, Edmonton, Lethbridge, London, Regina, Saskatoon, Windsor, Québec City) and every one of those small city groups fails at 100%. The national average is carried by the province-list majority.

Physiotherapy — 57/100 · 173 sites

The only industry with a 100/100 site in the whole study — and, by share, the most green sites (7%). London, Ontario is the stand-out clinic city so far: 5 sites, none failing, average 68/100. Laval (10 sites) sits at 56 with a 90 ceiling; Montreal (11 sites) falls to 49 with 64% failing.

Real estate — 55/100 · 110 sites

A tale of two lists. The main national sample runs mid-table with no disasters — best 99/100. The provincial waves are a warning: Lévis averages 36/100 with 9 of 10 sites failing, Rimouski 39, Victoria 35, and Vancouver just 23/100 — including the worst real-estate score in the entire study, 8/100. If your agent's site loads a photo gallery and a map before the phone number, this is why.

Optometry — 54/100 · 66 sites

The smallest sample, and the honest one: it's the most city-balanced list I have (Halifax, Victoria, Vancouver, Toronto, Winnipeg, Ottawa each 5–8 sites). Halifax leads at 67/100, Ottawa holds the niche's best single score (99/100). But 47% still fail — even a well-run niche can't fix a slow theme.

Dental — 50/100 · 234 sites

The largest sample — and the worst average. 51% of dental websites fail. The geography explains part of it: the country's slowest dental cities (Windsor 30/100, Vancouver 34, Ottawa 37, Winnipeg 37, Calgary 38, all 100% failing at city level; Toronto's dentists 42/100 at 84% failing) drag a niche that still manages bright spots — Gatineau's dentists are the only city group in Canada below 15% failure (61/100), and Mississauga's 52 dentist sites average 56 with a 98 best.

The honest fine print

This is a convenience sample of real audited businesses — not a census of industries. Sample sizes differ a lot (66 optometrists vs. 234 dentists), and so does geography: dental spans the most cities (including several 100%-failing ones), while HVAC and physiotherapy include many sites from province-level lists with no single city behind them. Optometry is the most city-balanced list, which makes its number the most "Canada-wide" of the five.

Every site is counted once, homepage only, real Google PageSpeed mobile test (simulated phone, slow 4G). Scores shift as sites are rebuilt — these are the numbers as of September 2026.
