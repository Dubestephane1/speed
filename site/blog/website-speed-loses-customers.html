Website speed loses customers: the numbers

September 23, 2026 · 5 min read

Speed isn't an IT detail. It's where customers decide. Here's the case in numbers — and in the scenario every local business owner knows.

The 3-second rule

Google's research on mobile behavior found that 53% of mobile visits are abandoned if a page takes more than 3 seconds to load. That study is from 2016 — on today's phones and connections, expectations are higher, not lower.

"Under 3 seconds" is the floor. Google's own technical target for when your main content appears is 2.5 seconds — and most of the local business sites I audit don't come close.

The midnight scenario

It's -30 °C and a furnace dies at midnight. The homeowner isn't browsing your site to "learn about you". They're on a phone, searching "heating repair near me", and they call the first site that loads. Your site taking 15 seconds means you were never in the race.

This isn't e-commerce theory. This is how emergency and local service work is found. Plumbers, HVAC, electricians, restaurants, clinics — the phone rings for the fastest page.

Real audits I run every week

These are anonymized results from businesses just like yours — the mobile test Google publishes itself:

  • A heating company in Québec City: 14/100 — 9.3 seconds to load its homepage on a phone
  • An air-exchange specialist in Sherbrooke: 26/100 — 15.8 seconds
  • A refrigeration company in Sherbrooke: 43/100 — 22.7 seconds
  • A thermostat specialist in Blainville: 44/100 — 18.3 seconds

Notice the pattern: the score is low and the load time is high — together. These aren't edge cases; they're the normal state of local business websites.

What a fix actually changes

It's not a redesign. It's the images, the scripts, and the server settings — usually a handful of changes. After the fixes, the same public test shows a much smaller number. The customer in the midnight scenario now calls you.
