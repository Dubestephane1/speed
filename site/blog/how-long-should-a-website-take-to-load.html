How long should a website take to load?

September 23, 2026 · 4 min read

Ask a web designer "how fast should my site be?" and you'll get a long story. Ask Google's own measurement, and you get three numbers that actually matter. Here they are, without the jargon.

1. LCP — when does the main thing appear?

Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) is the time until your page's main content shows up on a phone: your headline, your hero, your "call now" button. Google's target: under 2.5 seconds.

That's the number that hurt in my audits — local sites often sit at 8 to 23 seconds. That's not "a bit slow", it's a different universe.

2. INP — how quickly does the page respond to taps?

Interaction to Next Paint (INP) measures whether the page responds when a customer taps a menu, a button, a phone number. Target: under 200 milliseconds. If taps feel dead for a second, people assume the site is broken.

3. CLS — does the page jump around?

Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) measures elements moving after load — images popping in, banners sliding down, buttons shifting right as you're about to tap. Target: under 0.1. Layout jump is a top reason people fat-finger the wrong option and leave.

The score, explained in three bands

  • 90–100 (green): fast. This is the goal.
  • 50–89 (orange): workable but risky — under real phone conditions it usually feels slow.
  • 0–49 (red): this is where most local business sites actually sit. Customers are leaving.

"Green" is the finish line

You don't need a perfect 100. Once you're green with LCP under 2.5s, your site is in the same bucket as the fastest sites Google sees — and that's what affects customers and rankings. The last few points aren't worth losing your analytics or your design.
