What the international data shows

Across the 4 countries measured with at least 30 sites (Argentina, Brazil, Saudi Arabia, Singapore), the average score spans 1.3 points — from 62.8/100 in Saudi Arabia to 64.1/100 in Argentina.

A country’s wealth does not protect its small businesses from a slow website. The gap between countries is far smaller than the gap inside any one of them: in Brazil alone, the fastest site measured scored 100 and the slowest 17 — a spread of 83 points, against 1.3 between countries.

How to read this

  • Scores are Google PageSpeed Insights mobile performance scores, the same test your customers hit on a phone.
  • A city is only ranked once at least 10 of its sites returned a usable score. Smaller samples are labelled, never ranked.
  • Averages cover sites with a successful measurement only. Failed tests are counted separately, never averaged in as zero.
  • Every country and city page links its raw CSV, so you can check the numbers yourself.
  • 1 business has locations in more than one measured city (clareo.ca). One website means one measurement, but each city keeps its own result and every site is counted once in the totals above.
